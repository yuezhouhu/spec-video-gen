\begin{abstract}
Autoregressive video diffusion enables real-time streaming generation but
remains computationally demanding when deployed with high-quality
transformer models.
We propose \textbf{Reward-Guided Speculative Video Generation}
(RSVG), which adapts the speculative decoding paradigm from large
language models to block-based autoregressive video generation.
A compact drafter model generates candidate video blocks via four
denoising steps; an image quality router then decides, per block,
whether to accept the draft or re-generate with the full target model.
Three design choices are central: \emph{(i)} mandatory first-block
regeneration for compositional consistency, \emph{(ii)} worst-frame
quality scoring to detect per-frame artifacts masked by averages, and
\emph{(iii)} a globally adaptive threshold derived from a running percentile
of all observed draft scores.
On 200 prompts from MovieGenVideoBench at $832{\times}480$ resolution,
RSVG achieves \textbf{97.1\%} of target-only VisionReward quality
(0.0676 vs.\ 0.0696) at a \textbf{1.66$\times$ speedup} over the target
model, while improving over draft-only quality by $+23.1\%$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive video generation has recently emerged as a compelling
paradigm for efficient, streaming video synthesis.
Unlike conventional video diffusion models that generate all frames
jointly \citep{openaisora2024,moviegen2024}, autoregressive approaches
produce video block by block, conditioning each new block on previously
generated content through a shared key-value (KV) cache---mirroring the
autoregressive paradigm of large language models (LLMs).
This design eliminates exposure bias and enables streaming generation:
frames can be displayed as they are produced, rather than waiting for the
full sequence to complete.
Self-Forcing \citep{selfforcing2025} exemplifies this approach, training
a causal video diffusion transformer with self-generated conditioning that
achieves real-time video output on a single GPU.

Despite this structural efficiency advantage, state-of-the-art
autoregressive video models are built on 10B+ parameter transformers.
The Krea Realtime Video 14B model \citep{kreablog2025}, for instance,
requires a high-end NVIDIA B200 GPU to achieve real-time throughput;
on a commodity RTX A6000, generating a 27-frame clip takes over
112\,seconds.
At the same time, compact 1B-scale video models---such as
Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B \citep{wan2025}---run at roughly one-tenth of the
computational cost (36\,s on the same hardware) and produce lower but
still reasonable quality.
The central question is then: \emph{can we capture the speed of small
models while retaining the quality of large ones?}
Speculative decoding for LLMs \citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating}
offers a compelling blueprint: a small draft model proposes candidate
outputs, and the large target model is invoked only when necessary.
The block-by-block structure of autoregressive video generation is
especially well-suited to this paradigm---each generated block is a
self-contained unit that can be evaluated before being committed to the
KV cache, making per-block routing a natural design choice.

Making large and small models cooperate effectively is, however,
non-trivial.
Recent work has explored related ideas.
T-Stitch \citep{tstitch2024} and SRDiffusion \citep{srdiffusion2025}
both propose splitting the denoising trajectory between models at the
noise-step level: the small model handles certain steps and the large
model handles others.
MoDM \citep{modm2025} routes entire generation requests between models
at the serving system level based on a caching mechanism.
While effective in their respective settings, these approaches were not
designed for autoregressive video generation and carry meaningful
limitations.
T-Stitch and SRDiffusion rely on fixed, content-agnostic step splits---the
same routing rule applies to every block regardless of whether the draft
produces high- or low-quality output for that block, and they provide
no mechanism to detect and recover from bad drafts.
MoDM depends on cache hits to deliver speedup and offers no per-block
quality guarantee.
Critically, all three methods require step-level trajectory engineering or
system-level infrastructure, raising the barrier to deployment.

A further challenge distinguishes video from LLMs: classical speculative
decoding accepts or rejects drafts via exact token-probability comparisons
\citep{leviathan2023fast}.
Video blocks are continuous, high-dimensional spatiotemporal tensors with
no associated logit distribution, making token-level verification
inapplicable.
This leaves \emph{reward-guided speculative generation for autoregressive
video} as an open problem.

In this work, we propose \textbf{Reward-Guided Speculative Video
Generation} (\textbf{RSVG}), a training-free, plug-and-play framework
that requires no architectural changes to either the drafter or target.
For each video block, the drafter generates a candidate; an
image quality router then decides, per
block, whether to accept the draft or invoke the target for
regeneration.
Our key insight is that a plain image-quality signal, applied
block-by-block with a globally adaptive threshold, is sufficient to match
target-only quality without any step-level trajectory engineering---simplicity
is a feature, not a limitation.
On 200 MovieGenVideoBench prompts at $832{\times}480$ resolution, RSVG
achieves $97.1\%$ of target-only VisionReward quality ($0.0676$
vs.\ $0.0696$) at a $\mathbf{1.66\times}$ speedup, while improving over
draft-only by $+23.1\%$.
Importantly, RSVG is \emph{orthogonal} to step-level methods such as
T-Stitch or SRDiffusion, which can be directly applied to the target
model's generation steps within RSVG, providing a composable path to
further speedup.
Our main contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose RSVG, a training-free speculative generation framework
    for autoregressive video diffusion that routes each block between a
    drafter and a target based on image quality routing,
    achieving a $1.66\times$ speedup with $97.1\%$ quality retention.
  \item We identify three video-specific design choices critical to making
    reward-guided routing effective: mandatory first-block regeneration to
    anchor scene composition, worst-frame quality scoring to surface
    single-frame artifacts masked by block averages, and a globally
    adaptive running-percentile threshold that self-calibrates without any
    held-out calibration data.
  \item We demonstrate that RSVG is orthogonal to step-level acceleration
    methods (T-Stitch, SRDiffusion), and show that without any complex
    step-level trajectory engineering, a plain reward routing signal
    suffices to match large-model quality---establishing a new,
    simpler baseline for collaborative video generation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}

\subsection{Video Generation}

Diffusion-based video models have progressed rapidly from pixel-space
approaches \citep{yang2024video} to large latent transformer architectures
\citep{openaisora2024,moviegen2024}.
Wan 2.1 \citep{wan2025} provides open-source 1.3B and 14B video generation
models that serve as the backbone models in this work.
Inference efficiency has been improved through step distillation
\citep{yin2024onestepdiffusion}, flow-matching schedulers
\citep{liu2023flow}, and distributed parallelism \citep{he2024distrifusion},
though these approaches are orthogonal to---and composable with---the
routing-based framework we propose.

\subsection{Autoregressive Video Generation}

Self-Forcing \citep{selfforcing2025} trains a causal video diffusion
transformer $\Target$ to generate video block-by-block.
A video of $N$ frames is divided into $B$ blocks of $F$ frames each
($B{=}9$, $F{=}3$, $N{=}27$ in our setting).
At inference, block $b$ is generated by running $S{=}4$ denoising steps of
the transformer conditioned on the text prompt and a key-value (KV) cache
$\mathbf{K}_b$ accumulating representations of prior blocks $0,\ldots,b-1$.
This design eliminates exposure bias and enables streaming generation.
The system includes a smaller \emph{drafter} $\Drafter$ with the same
block-based autoregressive architecture but only 1.3B parameters vs.\ 14B
for $\Target$.
Both models use 4 denoising steps with shared schedule
$[1000, 937, 833, 625, 0]$.

\subsection{Hierarchical Video Generation}

A natural strategy to reduce inference cost is to assign different parts of
the generation process to models of different capacities.
T-Stitch \citep{tstitch2024} observes that diffusion models trained on the
same data distribution learn similar latent encodings and exploits this to
stitch trajectories across model sizes: a small model handles the
high-noise early denoising steps while a large model refines the output
in the low-noise later steps, achieving training-free acceleration without
quality loss.
SRDiffusion \citep{srdiffusion2025} adopts a complementary noise-level
split---the large model ``sketches'' global structure in the high-noise
phase while the small model ``renders'' visual details in the low-noise
phase---and reports up to $3\times$ speedup for Wan and $2\times$ for
CogVideoX.
MoDM \citep{modm2025} operates at the serving-system level, routing entire
generation requests to a small model on cache hits and to a large model
on misses, reducing average serving time by $2.5\times$.
While these methods demonstrate the value of model collaboration, they
rely on fixed, content-agnostic splits at the denoising step level and
were not designed for block-level autoregressive video generation.
RSVG is orthogonal to all three: T-Stitch and SRDiffusion can be directly
applied to the target model's generation steps within RSVG, providing a
composable path to further speedup.

\subsection{Speculative Decoding}

Speculative decoding \citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating}
accelerates LLM inference by pairing a small draft model with a large
target model: the draft generates candidate tokens autoregressively, and
the target verifies them in a single forward pass via rejection sampling,
preserving the target's output distribution exactly.
Subsequent work has explored learned drafters, tree-structured speculation,
and cross-architecture draft--target pairs.
RSD \citep{rsd2025} extends speculative decoding to chain-of-thought
reasoning by shifting acceptance from the token level to the step level:
a Process Reward Model (PRM) scores entire reasoning steps, accepting a
draft step when its PRM score exceeds a global threshold and otherwise
regenerating with the target.
RSVG draws direct inspiration from RSD but addresses the fundamental
difference that video blocks carry no discrete token distribution---making
exact rejection sampling inapplicable---and replaces the PRM with an
image quality model that scores decoded draft frames as a proxy for
block-level generation quality.

\section{Method: RSVG}
\label{sec:method}

Given text prompt $p$, drafter $\Drafter$ and target $\Target$,
we seek a routing policy $\pi: \mathbb{R} \to \{0,1\}$ that maps a
per-block quality score $\score_b \in \mathbb{R}$ to accept (1) or
reject (0), optimizing the trade-off between video quality and inference
speed.

\subsection{RSVG-Static}

\textbf{Inference flow.}
For each block $b$, $\Drafter$ runs $S$ denoising steps to produce a
candidate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_b$.
The drafter KV cache $\mathbf{K}^{\Drafter}$ is updated unconditionally,
ensuring $\Drafter$ always conditions on its own prior outputs.
The candidate is decoded by the VAE and scored by the router.
If $\score_b \geq \threshold$, the draft is \emph{accepted}:
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_b$ is committed to the target's KV cache
$\mathbf{K}^{\Target}$ and the decoded frames are emitted directly.
If \emph{rejected}, $\Target$ runs $S$ denoising steps from the same
initial noise to produce $\mathbf{x}^*_b$, updating $\mathbf{K}^{\Target}$.
The VAE decode cache is cloned before draft scoring and restored on
rejection to prevent temporal inconsistency across blocks.
In RSVG-Static the threshold $\threshold$ is a fixed scalar calibrated
offline.

\textbf{Worst-frame aggregation.}
The block quality score is the \emph{minimum} per-frame reward over the
$F$ decoded frames:
\begin{equation}
  \score_b = \min_{i=1}^{F}
  \ImageReward\!\left(\mathbf{f}^{(b)}_i,\, p\right).
  \label{eq:minscore}
\end{equation}
Here $\ImageReward(\mathbf{f}, p)$ denotes the reward of a single decoded
frame $\mathbf{f}$ given prompt $p$.
Using the minimum rather than the mean catches blocks with one severely
degraded frame---a visual artifact that average scoring would mask.
Empirically, accepted blocks achieve mean reward $0.768\pm0.705$
while rejected blocks score $-0.995\pm0.604$, a 1.76-point gap that
validates the router as a reliable routing signal.

\textbf{Force-reject the first block.}
Block $b=0$ is always regenerated by $\Target$, regardless of its draft
score.
Block 0 lacks any KV context from prior blocks and establishes the scene
composition, foreground subjects, and visual style that all subsequent
blocks inherit through the shared cache.
Accepting a draft at this position risks propagating irreversible layout
errors throughout the video.

\subsection{RSVG-Dynamic}

A fixed threshold requires a held-out calibration set and may fail to
generalize across prompts with differing score distributions.
RSVG-Dynamic removes this requirement by estimating the threshold on the
fly from scores accumulated during inference.

\textbf{Effective accept rate.}
The overall target accept rate $\alpha$ (fraction of all $B$ blocks
accepted) cannot be directly applied to scored blocks, because block 0 is
always rejected.
The effective accept rate for the remaining $B-1$ scored blocks is:
\begin{equation}
  \alphaeff = \frac{\alpha \cdot B}{B - 1}.
  \label{eq:effective-rate}
\end{equation}
With $\alpha{=}0.72$ and $B{=}9$, this gives $\alphaeff = 0.81$.

\textbf{Running-percentile threshold.}
Let $\mathcal{S}_t$ denote the pool of all min-frame scores accumulated
across all prompts and scored blocks up to time $t$.
The adaptive threshold is set at the $(1-\alphaeff)$-quantile of this
pool:
\begin{equation}
  \threshold_t = Q_{1 - \alphaeff}(\mathcal{S}_t),
  \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
where $Q_p$ denotes the empirical $p$-th quantile (linear interpolation).
Because scores accumulate across prompts, the threshold self-calibrates
to maintain the target accept rate without any held-out data.

\textbf{Gaussian warmup prior.}
When $|\mathcal{S}_t| < 5$, the empirical quantile is unreliable.
We substitute a Gaussian prior:
\begin{equation}
  \threshold_t = \mu_0 + \sigma_0 \cdot \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alphaeff),
  \label{eq:warmup}
\end{equation}
where $(\mu_0, \sigma_0) = (0.566, 1.071)$ are estimated from a small
development set and $\Phi^{-1}$ is the probit function.
After 5 observations the threshold transitions to the empirical quantile.

\begin{myalgorithm}[RSVG-Dynamic Inference]
\label{alg:rsvg}
\kwRequire text prompt $p$, blocks $B$, target rate $\alpha$,
drafter $\Drafter$, target $\Target$, router $\ImageReward$,
prior $(\mu_0, \sigma_0)$\\
\kwEnsure generated blocks $\mathbf{x}_{0:B-1}$

\Lstate{$\mathcal{S} \leftarrow [\,]$\Comment{running score pool}}
\Lstate{$\alphaeff \leftarrow \alpha \cdot B / (B - 1)$}
\Lstate{Init $\mathbf{K}^{\Drafter}$, $\mathbf{K}^{\Target}$, VAE cache $\mathbf{c}$}
\Lstate{\kwFor{$b = 0, 1, \ldots, B-1$}:}
\LstateII{$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_b \leftarrow \textsc{Denoise}(\Drafter, p, \mathbf{K}^{\Drafter})$;\quad update $\mathbf{K}^{\Drafter}$}
\LstateII{\kwIf{$b = 0$}: \textit{accept} $\leftarrow$ \textsc{False}\Comment{force-reject}}
\LstateII{\kwElse:}
\LstateIII{$\mathbf{c}' \leftarrow \mathbf{c}.\text{clone}()$\Comment{save VAE cache}}
\LstateIII{$\{\mathbf{f}_i\}, \mathbf{c}_{\text{new}} \leftarrow \VAE(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_b, \mathbf{c})$}
\LstateIII{$\score_b \leftarrow \min_i \ImageReward(\mathbf{f}_i, p)$;\quad $\mathcal{S}.\text{append}(\score_b)$}
\LstateIII{$\threshold \leftarrow Q_{1-\alphaeff}(\mathcal{S})$ if $|\mathcal{S}|\!\geq\!5$, else Eq.~(\ref{eq:warmup})}
\LstateIII{\textit{accept} $\leftarrow (\score_b \geq \threshold)$}
\LstateII{\kwIf{\textit{accept}}: $\mathbf{x}_b \leftarrow \hat{\mathbf{x}}_b$;\quad $\mathbf{c} \leftarrow \mathbf{c}_{\text{new}}$;\quad update $\mathbf{K}^{\Target}$}
\LstateII{\kwElse:}
\LstateIII{$\mathbf{c} \leftarrow \mathbf{c}'$\Comment{restore VAE cache}}
\LstateIII{$\mathbf{x}_b \leftarrow \textsc{Denoise}(\Target, p, \mathbf{K}^{\Target})$;\quad update $\mathbf{K}^{\Target}$}
\Lstate{\kwReturn $\mathbf{x}_{0:B-1}$}
\end{myalgorithm}

\paragraph{Computational cost.}
Let $t_{\Drafter}$ and $t_{\Target}$ denote per-block inference times.
RSVG runs $\Drafter$ on every block and $\Target$ only for block 0
(forced) plus fraction $1-\alphaeff$ of blocks $1\ldots B-1$.
At $\alphaeff = 0.734$ and a $10\times$ model-size ratio, the expected
speedup over target-only is:
\[
  \text{Speedup} \approx
  \frac{1}{\tfrac{1}{B} + \left(1-\tfrac{1}{B}\right)
  \!\left[\tfrac{1}{10} + (1-\alphaeff)\right]}
  \approx 1.6\times,
\]
consistent with the observed $1.66\times$.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
Target: Krea Realtime Video 14B, distilled from Wan2.1-T2V-14B
\citep{wan2025} via Self-Forcing \citep{selfforcing2025}.
Drafter: Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B fine-tuned with Distribution Matching
Distillation (DMD).
Both use 4 denoising steps with schedule $[1000, 937, 833, 625, 0]$
in bfloat16 precision.
ImageReward \citep{imagereward2024} runs on a separate GPU for
non-blocking evaluation.

\paragraph{Generation protocol.}
$B = 9$ blocks ($27$ frames, ${\approx}1.1$\,s at 24\,fps),
$832 \times 480$ resolution, seed = 42.
Hardware: two NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48\,GB) GPUs.

\paragraph{Prompts.}
200 prompts from MovieGenVideoBench \citep{moviegen2024}, covering
landscapes, animals, human activities, and cinematic footage.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
VisionReward \citep{visionreward2025}: 29 VQA questions spanning visual
quality, temporal consistency, motion naturalness, and text-video alignment.
Wall-clock inference time per video (excludes model loading and warmup).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\emph{Draft-only}: all blocks from the 1.3B drafter (max speed, min quality).
\emph{Target-only}: all blocks from the 14B target (min speed, max quality).

speedup}).
Compared to draft-only ($0.0549$), RSVG improves quality by

\subsection{Design Choice Analysis}

\paragraph{Force-rejecting the first block.}
Block 0 lacks any KV context and is most susceptible to layout errors and
prompt misalignment.
Forcing the target for block 0 anchors the video's scene composition and
provides a high-quality context for subsequent drafter predictions,
at the cost of one target forward pass per video.

\paragraph{Worst-frame vs.\ average scoring.}
The min-frame scoring in Eq.~(\ref{eq:minscore}) catches blocks with a
single artifact frame that average scoring would accept.
With $F{=}3$ frames per block, a single corrupted frame produces
visible temporal flickering; the min score reliably surfaces these cases.

\paragraph{Global vs.\ per-prompt threshold.}
A per-prompt threshold requires a calibration set and cannot adapt to
unseen distributions.
Our global running-percentile threshold requires no held-out data and
adapts within a single inference session; after $\sim$10 videos ($\sim$80
scored blocks), the empirical quantile is well-estimated.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Distributional bias.}
Unlike exact-rejection LLM speculative decoding, RSVG accepts a
distributional shift toward the drafter; the $-2.9\%$ VisionReward gap
reflects this.
A lower accept rate reduces the gap at the cost of speedup.

\textbf{ImageReward as a proxy.}
ImageReward was trained on text-image pairs and evaluates frames
independently, missing temporal consistency and motion quality.
A dedicated video-block quality model would improve the routing signal.

\textbf{Wasted draft computation.}
For the 34.8\% rejected blocks (including forced block-0 rejections),
the drafter forward pass and VAE decode are wasted computation.
Batching or speculative VAE decoding could reduce this overhead.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented RSVG, a reward-guided speculative video generation framework
for autoregressive video diffusion that achieves 97.1\% of target quality
at 1.66$\times$ speedup.
The key innovations---forced first-block regeneration, worst-frame quality
scoring, and a globally adaptive percentile threshold---address the specific
challenges of block-level video speculative decoding.
The clear separation in router scores between accepted and rejected
drafts (mean gap 1.76) validates that an image-level quality model serves
as a reliable routing signal for video blocks with minimal calibration.
RSVG can be applied to any Self-Forcing-style autoregressive video
model with a drafter-target pair, opening the door to reward-guided
inference-time compute allocation for video generation.

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{app:impl}

\paragraph{Model architecture.}
Target: Krea Realtime Video 14B (\texttt{krea-realtime-video-14b.safetensors}),
bfloat16.
Drafter: DMD fine-tuned Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (\texttt{self\_forcing\_dmd.pt}).
Both use causal attention with KV caching via RoPE positional embeddings.

\paragraph{Denoising schedule.}
$\mathbf{t} = [1000, 937, 833, 625, 0]$; 4 steps per block.
Guidance scale 3.0, timestep shift 5.0.

\paragraph{VAE.}
Causal VAE decoder (\texttt{Wan2.1\_VAE.pth}); 3 frames per block.
Decode cache is cloned before draft scoring and restored on rejection.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.}
$\alpha = 0.72$, warmup prior $(\mu_0, \sigma_0) = (0.566, 1.071)$,
warmup period 5 scored blocks.

\paragraph{Hardware.}
Two NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48\,GB) GPUs.
GPU 0: diffusion transformer (target + drafter).
GPU 1: text encoder (UMT5-XXL), VAE, ImageReward.
CUDA streams overlap transfer and compute.
